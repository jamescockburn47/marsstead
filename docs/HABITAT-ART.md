# Inhabited habitat art — September 2026

The 8 × 8 metre room footprint still comes from completed `burrow.cells`. Four real beds now occupy 17.4 square metres (formerly 8.265), with two long growing banks, a clear cross-room aisle, trellises, drip lines and suspended baskets. Seven crop forms use distinct structures: lettuce rosettes, fruiting tomato, climbing bean, grasses, herbs, seedlings and hanging vines. Leaves are thin curved meshes with age/colour/posture variation, lobed margins and procedural veins. Geometry is merged per room/material; plants do not add draw calls per leaf, textures, transmission passes or lights. The planted bed collisions come from the same obstacle definitions as rendering.

Bunks use compressed superellipsoid pillows and mattresses, draped fold geometry, rough woven materials, a throw, rug and books. These replace the former rectangular bedding blocks.

Research-informed choices: the University of Calgary's plant graphics work identifies silhouette, curved posture and gradual feature variation as key visual controls; those drive these small bounded generators. [Positional information in plant models](https://www.algorithmicbotany.org/papers/positional.sig2001.html). Leaf shape should vary structurally rather than by scaling a repeated ellipsoid: [leaf development and form](https://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/leaves2017.html). Materials follow Three's metallic/roughness model with high roughness and zero metalness for foliage/textiles: [MeshStandardMaterial](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshStandardMaterial.html).

Evidence: `node scripts/verify-habitat.mjs` covers bounds, shared collisions, clear doors, crop structures, leaf curvature, fabric geometry, disposal and material batching. `node scripts/shots-habitat-art.mjs` renders the actual HabitatLayer in WebGL and writes `media/habitat-art/garden.png` and `bunk.png`; both visually inspected. The fixture has no player state and no external assets. Full game-browser integration remains the lead's final check.
